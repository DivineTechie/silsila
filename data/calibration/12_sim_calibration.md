## 3. Ahmed, Saleem, Modell, Petrou, NEJM 2002;347(15):1162-8
PMID 12374877, doi 10.1056/NEJMsa013234. Full text read from https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/7166/1/7166.pdf (downloaded by curl; the discovery-pp URL returned 403 to WebFetch). Setting: Armed Forces Institute of Pathology, Rawalpindi.
- Selection: voluntary participation and availability of many family members. 16 index families approached (15 hemoglobin disorder + 1 carrier-family per Methods; 10 agreed), 8 control families approached (5 agreed). Note the paper is internally inconsistent: Methods say "15 families of children + 1 carrier family + 8 controls"; Results say 16 index approached/10 tested and 5 of 8 controls; abstract says 15 families (10+5). Follow-up: abstract "two years", Results text "four years", 8 of 10 families.
- Design: key members meet, 3-generation pedigree drawn, testing at home. Whole-family screening, ~70% of living index-family members tested. 350 consecutive antenatal women as comparison group.
- Study families: 1,455 living members in 15 families (mean 97; index mean 84.6, range 48-199; control mean 121.8, range 72-152); 338 married couples: 44% consanguineous (second cousins or closer), 50% same biradri, 6% unrelated (index 43/50/7; control 45/51/4); 54% of members unmarried.
- Results: index families 846 living, 591 tested (70%), 183 carriers (31%) of whom 97 unmarried, 86 married; 214 married couples in index families (126 tested); among 214 couples 86 carriers, 34 married to a carrier, 17 couples both carriers (9 parents of index case, 6 identified by history, 2 by testing). 11 of 17 at-risk couples closely consanguineous, 6 related only through biradri. 28 affected children (15 alive, 13 dead). Controls: 609 living, 397 tested, 0 carriers. Antenatal: 350 women, 17 carriers (5%), 0 at-risk couples. Population carrier frequency used: ~5% beta-thal; 0.5-1% HbS or HbE. Paper's own theory: 31% x 5% = ~1.6% chance a relative enters an at-risk marriage; ~10% of couples in affected families at risk; carriers 20-30% chance.
- Follow-up: 7 new marriages/engagements, 4 consanguineous couples known not at risk; 6 couples at risk had 7 prenatal diagnoses; 1 at-risk couple declined PND and had an affected child. Abstract: 7 of 8 new marriages known not at risk. Per-family table in the YAML.

## 5. Analytic validation values
- First-cousin offspring excess risk: Bennett et al. 2002, J Genet Couns 2002, pp. 97-119, PMID 26141656 (Europe PMC lists 26141656 for this record), doi 10.1023/a:1014593404915. Verified in University of Washington release (https://www.washington.edu/news/2002/04/03/first-cousins-face-lower-risk-of-having-children-with-genetic-conditions-than-is-widely-perceived): additional risk 1.7 to 2.8 percent for first-cousin unions, over a population base risk (example 5%). Original full text not read. Bennett 2021 focused revision: PMID 34309119 (abstract only).
- Kinship coefficient (first cousin 1/8, F of offspring 1/16 = 0.0625; double first cousin F = 1/8; second cousin F = 1/64 = 0.0156) is a mathematical identity, not a literature number. Bittles & Black define consanguineous as F >= 0.0156. P(first cousin of a known carrier is a carrier) ~ 1/8 + (7/8) q_pop is a derivation (shared-allele probability 1/8 per specified allele pathway, to be confirmed by Monte Carlo), NOT a cited value; empirical anchor: Ahmed 2002 31% carriers among relatives (all degrees) vs 5% population.
- UAE F anchors: 0.0222 (Al-Gazali 1997) and 0.022 (Denic 2013).
